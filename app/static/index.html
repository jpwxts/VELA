        Vela divides the area into grid squares. Each square on one day counts once. The weekly percentage estimates how often those squares showed burning activity on the days with usable estimates.

        How to read this

        A square observed on Monday and Tuesday counts twice, once for each day. The value measures activity frequency; it is not the percentage of land burned or a count of separate fires.

            What did the satellite see?

            Satellites report heat detections when their sensors see a hot pixel during a pass. Those readings are evidence of heat, not a wildfire count.

            Technical details

            Vela keeps each satellite’s readings identifiable, then groups nearby detections into 5 km observation areas so you can compare what more than one spacecraft saw.

            How is fire danger calculated?

            Fire danger here is a published weather-based index for the forecast day, separate from satellite heat detections. It is not a wildfire probability.

            Technical details

            Vela reads the Copernicus GWIS ECMWF Fire Weather Index at the forecast point when the WMS layer returns a number. For Southeast Asia, the ASMC / Malaysian Meteorological Department Fire Weather Index map is linked as a regional reference only; Vela never infers a rating from map colors.

            What would confirm a wildfire?

            An official wildfire or fire report, on-the-ground confirmation, or a responsible agency bulletin confirms a wildfire. Satellite heat and fire-danger ratings alone do not.

            Technical details

            Weekly activity estimates measure how often grid squares showed burning activity on days with usable observations or model estimates. Development validation compares those estimates with held-out satellite observations; independent final testing is recorded separately when completed.
